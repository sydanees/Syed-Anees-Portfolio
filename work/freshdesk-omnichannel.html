Choosing the problem

The brief I was handed was to bring live channels, WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram, into Freshdesk. That's a feature-parity ask, and I treated it as a hypothesis, not a spec.

I ran a diagnostic instead of a status update: customer interviews with agents and team leads, a direct audit of what native apps support that Freshdesk couldn't render, a UX audit of every existing channel, a gap analysis against what live-channel support requires, and a competitive teardown against Zendesk, Intercom and Sprout Social.

The stated problem was "missing features." The audit data pointed somewhere else: every channel already in Freshdesk had been designed in isolation, with no shared contract between them. Feature parity would have added a fourth inconsistent channel to three inconsistent ones already live. The actual problem was structural: there was no definition of what "a channel in Freshdesk" should be, so nothing could scale.

"We prefer Freshchat's channel switching experience but most of the tickets assigned to us are in Freshdesk." — Agent, customer interview
"Managing two different tools for the same account feels inefficient. I just want one place to set up channels and track results." — Admin, customer interview

Agents had already experienced a good version of this, just in the wrong product. Admins were paying a duplicate-setup tax for the same reason. The goal reframed again: not "design live-channel UX," but meet a bar agents already trusted and remove the admin's duplicate work, via a contract every future channel could reuse.

Key decisions

One reply editor, not two. Freshdesk ran separate editors for messaging versus social channels, different behavior, different mental model per conversation type. I collapsed both into a single editor with channel-source icon and comment/DM mode switching, so "how do I reply" stopped being a channel-specific question.

Two pillars, not N channel-specific designs. Instead of designing channel by channel, I split the problem into two reusable contracts: Admin Experience (pre-setup, setup, post-setup) and Agent Experience (rendering, reply behavior, switching). A new channel implements the contract; it doesn't get a new design.

Ship the defensible version, name the gap. The full progressive-disclosure redesign for Facebook comment hierarchy didn't fit the release window. I shipped the readability improvements that did fit and documented the fuller version as a scoped, named follow-up, rather than block the release or ship a silently worse version.
