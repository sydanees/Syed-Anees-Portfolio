# Syed Anees — Portfolio

## Mission

Build a memorable Staff Product Designer portfolio that helps me present my work to major technology and AI companies, including international opportunities in KSA, UAE, Europe, New Zealand and Australia.

The portfolio is the product. The hiring manager is the user. My career is the outcome.

Positioning:
> I'm Syed Anees, a Product Designer. I make expensive problems disappear.

Do not rely on the phrase alone. Demonstrate the positioning through the work, storytelling, interaction design and product thinking.

## Audience

Primary:
- Design Managers
- Design Directors / Design Leadership
- Staff/Principal designers
- Product leaders
- Recruiters / hiring managers at major technology and AI companies

Secondary future use:
- Digital products
- Figma/design resources
- Personal brand
- Consulting/freelance opportunities

## Visual direction

Minimal + modern + offbeat + editorial + human + confident + memorable.

Prefer:
- Strong typography
- Excellent composition
- Negative space
- Editorial rhythm
- Specificity
- Human photography
- Meaningful motion
- Subtle unexpected details
- Strong product storytelling

Avoid:
- Generic AI-generated portfolio aesthetics
- Excessive gradients
- Glassmorphism
- Generic rounded card grids
- Decorative 3D objects without purpose
- Excessive pills/badges
- Animation everywhere
- Generic SaaS visuals
- Template-like layouts
- Empty buzzwords

The site should feel art-directed, not AI-assembled.

## Core information architecture

Initial homepage:
1. Hero
2. Selected Work
3. About
4. Testimonials
5. Contact
6. Footer

Case studies should have dedicated routes/pages and use a reusable template.

## Staff-level signals

The portfolio should naturally demonstrate:
- Systems thinking
- Product thinking
- Strategic decision making
- Handling ambiguity
- Leadership and influence
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Business/user outcomes
- Design craft
- AI/product experience where supported by real work

Never invent metrics, testimonials, achievements, responsibilities or outcomes.

Use clearly marked placeholders when real content is missing.

## Motion

Motion should serve:
- Orientation
- Hierarchy
- Storytelling
- Feedback
- Personality

Prefer one exceptional signature interaction over many decorative animations.

Motion must remain performant, accessible and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.

## Skills

Before major implementation work, inspect available Claude Code skills.

Use:
- Ponytail when it materially improves implementation efficiency, reduces repetitive context/tool work, or helps keep the coding loop lean.
- Taste when making or reviewing visual/design decisions, especially to detect generic AI-slop patterns.

Do not invoke skills mechanically. Do not sacrifice quality for token savings.

## Coding principles

- Investigate the existing repository before changing it.
- Reuse existing components and dependencies where sensible.
- Make targeted edits.
- Avoid unnecessary abstractions.
- Don't rebuild working systems without a reason.
- Keep content separate from presentation where practical.
- Optimize images and animations.
- Build responsive behavior intentionally.
- Preserve accessibility.
- Prefer simple maintainable solutions.

## Working style

Work incrementally.

For substantial tasks:
1. Inspect relevant files.
2. Make the smallest coherent change.
3. Run the relevant checks/build.
4. Inspect the result.
5. Continue only when the result is sound.

Do not repeatedly reread unchanged files or regenerate large sections unnecessarily.

When the request is clear, implement rather than merely suggesting.

Ask a question only when missing information materially affects the result.

## Design review

After major visual sections, evaluate:
- Does this feel intentional?
- Does this feel like Syed rather than a template?
- Is the hierarchy obvious?
- Is the interaction meaningful?
- Would this blend into 100 AI-generated portfolios?
- Can anything be removed?
- Does the work demonstrate Staff-level thinking?

If it feels generic, improve the underlying design decision rather than adding random novelty.

## Important constraint

The website should feel like a personal product, not a design agency site, SaaS landing page, or AI-generated portfolio template.
